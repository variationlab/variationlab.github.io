Elena L. Glassman

Assistant Professor, Computer Science

Human-computer interaction, AI-resilient interfaces, Human-AI collaboration

Elena L. Glassman is an Assistant Professor of Computer Science at the Harvard John A. Paulson School of Engineering & Applied Sciences, specializing in human-computer interaction. Prior to that, she was a postdoctoral scholar at UC Berkeley, and obtained a BS, MEng, and PhD in Electrical Engineering and Computer Science from MIT. She has been named a Stanley A. Marks & William H. Marks Professor at the Radcliffe Institute for Advanced Study and a National Academy of Sciences Kavli Fellow. Her work has been funded by the NSF, private industry, the Berkeley Institute for Data Science, and the Sloan Research Fellowship. This work has received Best Paper and Honorable Mention awards at top-tier human-computer interaction research venues.

Trevor DePodesta

PhD Student, Computer Science

LLM interpretability, Human-AI interaction, Data visualization

Hey! 👋 My name is Trevor, and I’m a PhD Candidate in Computer Science. Prior to joining the Variation Lab, I spent four years co-advised by profs. Martin Wattenberg and Fernanda Viegas in the Insight & Interaction Lab, with a research focus at the intersection of interpretability, visualization, and human-centered design. Much of my research asks what people need to see, steer, or verify to effectively engage with interactive AI systems, and builds interfaces that pair language with a visible, adjustable view of the state that shapes their results. A former Resident Tutor, I did my undergraduate and masters degrees at Harvard, where I became the College’s first ever AI degree holder with an AB in Ethical Human-AI Interaction. I also co-teach ES 297, SEAS’ Scientific Writing Practicum every semester—interested graduate students (or advanced undergraduates working on a thesis) should feel free to reach out about joining our waitlists. Beyond my time at SEAS, I have held research affiliations with the Harvard Radcliffe Institute, the Belfer Center Intelligence Project at Harvard Kennedy School, and the Berkman Klein Center for Internet and Society at Harvard Law, where I now run the Student Leaders in AI program.

Chau Vu

PhD Student, Computer Science

Human-AI interaction, Creativity support, Personal informatics

I am Chau, a first-year PhD student in Computer Science at SEAS! I study how to design cognitively inviting tools that help people engage with complex work while leaving room for their own interpretation and critical thought. My previous projects range from tools for creative practice, AI-assisted programming, navigating research literature, to understanding critical personal health or legal data. Across these areas, my work aims to support both people's productivity and capacity to question and act on matters that affect their lives. Always open to hear from you, whether you have a question or an idea we could collaborate on! 😊
